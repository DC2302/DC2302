# Creative Brief: We Don't Hand You a Binder (OSO — what we actually do)

**Auto-mode decisions, one line each — stated up front because every downstream agent inherits them:**

- **OSO, not OCD.** Selling the parent brand (business efficiency and optimization consulting), because this is the pinned introduction to OSO's profile, the two sibling videos already built an OSO consulting audience, and OCD already has its own launch piece (`projects/ocd-video-tool-launch/`) — putting "obsessively consistent branding" in the same 40 seconds would sell neither.
- **Goal: brand-trust.** The two prior OSO videos are both lead-gen consultation ads; a third would leave the profile with no piece that answers "who are you and what do you sell," which is exactly the job of a pinned intro running to cold viewers.
- **Same audience as the siblings.** Deliberate: this video is the *answer* to the two problem-first pieces, so a viewer who came from "Sleepless Nights" or "Flying Blind" lands somewhere that explains the fix.
- **40s / 4 blocks.** Matches the sibling runtime so the pinned trio reads as one series, and sits inside the playbook's 20–45s target rather than stretching to 50s.
- **Hook attacks the category, not the customer.** A "what we do" video only earns a real hook by opening with what we *don't* do.

---

- **Goal:** brand-trust (primary). Single goal, single CTA. The idea as given contains two possible campaigns — OSO consulting and the OCD "obsessively consistent branding" sub-brand — and this brief picks OSO consulting; OCD is out of scope for this cut.

- **Audience:** The blue-collar field-service business owner from the two prior OSO videos — trucks, crews, job sites, roughly 5–50 people — who has already admitted the back office is a mess and is now at the next question: *"Okay, so who actually fixes this, and what would they even do?"* Secondary read on LinkedIn: the same operator plus the ops manager or partner who has to justify bringing someone in.

- **Core promise:** OSO doesn't advise your business — it rebuilds the three systems it runs on (how the work runs, how you get found, how you get paid) and installs them with your crew, so it still works after we leave.

- **Hook (first 2 seconds):**
  1. **[STRONGEST]** A thick three-ring binder drops onto a steel desk with a hard thud, dust punching up through an ice-blue shaft of light. On-screen text: **"This is what most consultants leave you with."** Then a chrome grizzly paw shoves it off the desk, out of frame. — Physical, motion-and-sound pattern interrupt that is legible at thumbnail size (the playbook makes the first frame the TikTok cover), it pre-empts this audience's number-one objection — *consultants are expensive people who talk* — before making a single claim, it is built from OSO's own verbatim positioning ("installed with your team, not handed over in a binder") rather than invented, and it is tonally distinct from both siblings: "Sleepless Nights" was interior and empathetic, "Flying Blind" was diagnostic and mechanical, this one is confrontational and physical. Critically, it lets an about-us video open by saying what we *don't* do, which is the only way this format gets a real hook.
  2. Split-second triptych, three hard cuts on the beat: a dispatch board filling, a phone lighting up with an inbound call, an invoice stamping PAID — then freeze. On-screen text: **"Three things run a field-service business. We rebuild all three."** — Fast and concrete, sets the arc up perfectly, but it opens on abstraction-by-montage rather than a single stopping image, and reads weaker as a static cover frame.
  3. A signed field ticket at the tailgate, cut straight to an invoice going out before the crew is off the clock. On-screen text: **"Signed on site. Invoiced before they clock out."** — Hyper-concrete mini-demo of OSO Command and a genuinely strong hook, but it leads with only one of the three pillars, so it mis-sets expectations for a "what we do" piece, and the timing is an illustration rather than a guaranteed SLA (see Risks). Held in reserve as a beat-2 image.

- **Message arc:** *(4 beats → 4 blocks × 10s; block 4 carries the CTA and reserves its final seconds for the logo end-card, matching both sibling videos)*
  1. **Hook — the binder (0–10s):** The binder lands, the dust flies, the paw sweeps it off the desk. The category's default deliverable is a document that sits on a shelf. That is not this.
  2. **What we actually do — the triad (10–20s):** Three hard cuts, one line each, no service menus. Fix how the business **runs** (assessment, SOPs, process, compliance, automation, the numbers). Fix how it gets **found** (website, SEO, Google Maps, social, branding, follow-up). Fix how it gets **paid** (OSO Command — dispatch to field ticket to invoice to money in the account, one platform). The takeaway to burn in is three words: **Runs. Found. Paid.**
  3. **Why it matters — installed, not delivered (20–30s):** The credibility beat and the real differentiator. We build it *with your crew*, in your business, on your jobs — so the SOP is the way your people actually work, not a PDF nobody opens. When we leave, it keeps running. This is what separates OSO from a consultant and from software you bought and never rolled out.
  4. **CTA + end-card (30–40s):** Land the brand line — "Smarter workflows. Stronger bottom line." — then the single ask: see the whole system at osoptimization.com/en. Final seconds reserved for the OSO shield end-card (handled in post; see Risks re: which mark).

- **CTA:** **"See the whole system — osoptimization.com/en"** (spoken: "See the whole system at osoptimization dot com"). One ask, no second ask. The free 30-minute consultation and the phone number both live one click away on that page, so no conversion is lost by keeping the video's ask soft — and the social-media-manager may carry "free 30-minute consultation" in caption copy without adding a competing on-screen CTA. *Recommend writing this back to `brand/brand-profile.md` → Standing CTAs → Brand trust, currently TBD; the sibling briefs already recommend "Book a 30-minute consultation" for the Lead-generation row.*

- **Platforms:** All five are in scope off one 9:16 master (1080×1920), per the playbook's one-master-five-packages rule.
  - **Primary: Facebook Reels, 9:16** — the playbook names it as the surface where the owner-operator audience over-indexes, which is literally OSO's buyer; it is also the most forgiving surface for a slightly more explanatory cut and the only one of the five where the CTA URL is clickable directly in the caption, which matters when the CTA *is* a URL.
  - **Secondary: TikTok** (still posted first per the default plan, for the fastest retention read), **Instagram Reels** (link sticker + bio link, cover pinned manually to the hook frame), **LinkedIn** (first-person operator register, CTA link in the first comment), **YouTube Shorts** (title = the hook line verbatim, full URL in the description).
  - **No 4:5 and no 16:9 variant.** LinkedIn is not the primary platform, so per the playbook the 4:5 alt is not produced; there is no main-YouTube-channel requirement, so 16:9 is skipped.
  - **Pin it.** This is the profile-intro asset: pin on TikTok and Instagram, set as the featured video on LinkedIn and the channel trailer on YouTube.

- **Length:** **40 seconds — 4 blocks × 10s.** Chosen over 50s/5 blocks even though three pillars would each take a block: the discipline of compressing them into one rhythmic triad is precisely what keeps this from becoming the corporate about-us it is at risk of being, "Runs. Found. Paid." is more memorable than three separate 10s service segments, and 40s sits inside the playbook's 20–45s target instead of stretching TikTok's 15–35s sweet spot. It also matches "Sleepless Nights" and "Flying Blind" exactly, so the pinned set reads as a series. Well under 60s, so all five platforms stay eligible off the single master.

- **Tone notes:** Confident per the brand voice, but the specific register here is **proof-of-work — foreman energy**. The siblings were night-time empathy and dusk-time diagnosis; this one is daylight, shop-floor, sleeves-up: things being physically installed, panels switched on, a board filling up, work getting finished. Declarative and unhedged — an operator describing what he builds, not a firm describing its capabilities. Efficiency language over agency language ("install," "system," "runs," "paid," "crew"). The OSO Grizzly's characterization here is **the one doing the work** — not the guardian of "Sleepless Nights," not the calibrator of "Flying Blind." Style lock applies unchanged: ink-navy/steel environments, chrome detail, electric ice-blue glow, esports-badge contrast. No hype verbs — never "transform," "revolutionize," "10x," or "unlock." Land "Smarter workflows. Stronger bottom line." at the payoff, once.

- **Proof points / facts used:** All first-party, taken from OSO's own site — https://www.osoptimization.com/en, fetched 2026-08-02. No third-party research was needed and none was used, because this video makes no market, trend, or comparative claim that would require grounding; no statistic appears in it.
  - **Operations Consulting — "Fix how the business runs":** operational assessment, SOP development, process implementation, workflow optimization, compliance integration, AI & automation, tech stack consulting, data & reporting, growth & exit readiness.
  - **Digital Services — "Fix how the business is found":** website design & development, SEO & content, Google Business Profile & Maps, social media management, branding & logo design, marketing automation — "the reputation infrastructure that works before the phone rings."
  - **OSO Command — "Field tickets to invoices to paid. One platform.":** dispatch, bilingual field tickets, same-day invoicing, live GPS, job costing, QuickBooks integration.
  - **The differentiator line, verbatim from the site:** "installed with your team, not handed over in a binder" — this is the source of both the hook and beat 3, and it is OSO's own language, not an invention.
  - Standing offers on the page for the CTA destination: "Book your free 30-minute consultation" and "Call 726-610-5-OSO."

- **Risks:**
  - **Service-menu drift is the big one.** Nine consulting services, six digital services, and a platform are documented above as *grounding for the writer*, not as a list to read aloud. Beat 2 is three lines, one per pillar. If the script starts enumerating, the video becomes the corporate about-us this brief exists to avoid.
  - **End-card mark conflict.** The logo in `brand/brand-profile.md` is the OSO-OCD badge with film strip and clapperboard — a content-production mark. This is a parent-brand consulting video; a clapperboard on the end-card actively mis-signals the offer. Use the OSO parent mark if one exists; otherwise the grizzly shield with the film-strip/clapperboard elements omitted. Flagging for the producer to resolve rather than defaulting.
  - **The binder hook must stay aimed at the artifact, not at people.** "This is what most consultants leave you with" is a contrast with a deliverable format. It must never become a swipe at named competitors, and the narration should not editorialize about consultants being useless — OSO *is* a consultancy.
  - **OSO Command timing claims.** The site says "same-day invoicing." Do not escalate to "instant," "in minutes," or a specific number of minutes — hook option 3 ("invoiced before they clock out") is an illustration and, if it is used anywhere, must stay a depiction rather than a stated guarantee.
  - **"It keeps running after we leave"** is the strongest line in the piece and also the least verifiable. Keep it as a description of how OSO works (installed with the team, in their workflow) rather than a promised outcome or a durability guarantee.
  - **Two-brand confusion.** OCD does not appear in this video at all — no "obsessively consistent branding" tagline, no content-production references. One identity on screen.
  - **Bilingual scope.** "Bilingual field tickets" is a real product feature and may be shown as an on-screen detail, but the narrator voice is locked to English and this master stays English; a Spanish cut is a separate future job, not a mixed-language moment here.
  - **Muted playback and safe zone.** The hook line is the TikTok cover and must be legible at thumbnail size, and per the playbook narration can never be the sole carrier — burn "Runs. Found. Paid." and the URL on screen, keeping all text out of the top ~12% and bottom ~20%.
